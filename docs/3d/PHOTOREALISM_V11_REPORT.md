# Photorealism V11 — Real-Asset Integration, World Coherence, Visual Realism

> Pass goal: stop asking "do we have valid GLBs?" and start asking "does the
> whole rendered site look like one real, professionally photographed Indian
> construction environment?"

## A note on the numbers in this document

This project already distinguishes between *structural statistics* and
*realism*, and this report keeps that distinction. `VISUAL_ASSET_QA.md` scores
component separation, normal richness and mass distribution — it is a defect
detector. It cannot tell you whether a building looks real.

So the scores below are **judgements from rendered evidence**, not tool output.
Each one is backed by a frame in `.qa/world/` or `.qa/v11/` that I looked at.
Where a target was not met, it says so rather than rounding up. The automated
suites (`assets:build`, `qa:daylight`, `qa:placement`, `qa:shots`, `typecheck`,
`build`) are reported separately as pass/fail, because that is what they are.

## 3. Hero — architectural changes

The V10 hero was good detail on a bad plan. Podium, tower and crown were one
22 × 16 m extrusion, so at any distance where the whole building was visible it
read as a single rectangular mass with windows on it. That is a plan problem,
and bevels, noise and triangles cannot fix it.

Five moves, all dimensioned so they could be built:

1. **The plan is an L.** A four-storey service wing runs off the north-east
   corner carrying the secondary stair and shafts, giving the building a
   re-entrant corner and a stepped profile in two directions.
2. **The podium is genuinely a base.** 29.2 × 33.1 m against a 22 × 16 m tower,
   two commercial levels of mullioned glazing with stall risers, its own deep
   cornice, and a planted roof terrace on the leftover footprint.
3. **A real setback at level 8.** The upper block loses 3.4 m of depth on the
   road side. This creates an occupied terrace with planters and a pergola, and
   puts a hard horizontal shadow across the lower tower.
4. **The road elevation is grouped, not repeated.** Lower tower: 5 wide bays
   with balconies on the flanks. A two-storey recessed shadow band. Then the
   setback, then 7 narrow bays with fins and french balconies above. Reading up
   the building you pass through changes.
5. **The crown is a service level**, with a deeper soffit, louvred plant and the
   lift overrun standing proud — a profile rather than a lid.

**Entrance (§13):** forecourt paving, three steps *and* an accessible ramp with
a handrail, columns with base and capital carrying the canopy, a double-height
glazed lobby with transom and mullions, doors as two pairs of leaves with
meeting stiles and pull handles, signage on brackets, bollards.

Evidence: `.qa/world/01-build.png`, `.qa/v11/hero-far-AFTER.png`,
`.qa/v11/hero-entrance-AFTER.png`.

## 4. Residential

Rebuilt so it reads as housing, per §14's "must not look like the hero scaled
down". Three moves: **stilt parking** at ground level (open, you see through
the building — the single most recognisable feature of the type, and something
the hero deliberately does not do); **continuous full-width balconies on every
floor** with planters and washing lines, rather than occasional accents; and a
**stair core** rising past the parapet into a head house.

One bug found and fixed while checking it: the block sits at x = +50, east of
the corridor, but was unrotated — so it presented its blank service flank to
every camera that passed. Now rotated a quarter turn to face the approach.

Evidence: `.qa/world/05-service-residential.png`.

## 5. Car integration and environmental context

**The grounding bug.** Parked cars were placed at x = ±6.6 against a constant
named `ROAD_EDGE = 8.25`. But 8.25 is the *road reserve* boundary — the extent
of highway land. The carriageway ends at 3.75 m and the metalled shoulder at
5.65 m. Every parked car in the scene was a metre out on the grass verge with
its far wheels over the drain.

Fixed by introducing `SHOULDER_EDGE` (5.65) and measuring bays from it, and by
seating each car with `carriagewayHeight()` so tyres follow the road's camber
and grade instead of sitting at a flat y = 0.

**The plausibility problem (§4, §6).** The external car is a *concept
supercar*: 1.15 m tall, where a road car is 1.4–1.9 m. It was the hero vehicle
at the very first parking bay. This is the same failure as the Victorian lamp
post — technically valid, contextually wrong.

Rather than deleting a licensed, well-made asset, the pipeline now measures the
problem. `spec.mjs` height floor tightened from 1.10 m to the brief's own
figure; assets outside their envelope on two or more axes are scored
**atypical**; `stageByRealism()` orders pools so typical models take the
foreground and atypical ones fall to the back. The supercar still appears —
twice, in the midground and at the far end of the corridor, where a distinctive
silhouette is an asset rather than a distraction.

Evidence: `.qa/v11/car-close.png` (before), `.qa/v11/car-foreground.png`
(after).

## 6. Bridge, warehouse, shed

**Bridge:** the deck, girders, piers with cutwaters, pier caps, bearings,
abutments, wing walls, arch barrel and spandrel columns were all already
present and correct. The actual defect was the approach embankments — two
featureless 12 × 15 × 19 m stone boxes that filled half the infrastructure
beat as a blank white slab. Rebuilt as battered retaining walls with
counterfort ribs, coping continuing the parapet line, weep holes and graded
verges, with the fill set inside them.

**Warehouse and shed:** inspected and left alone. Both already carry their
industrial logic. §33 says not to rebuild passing assets to show activity.

Evidence: `.qa/world/06-service-infrastructure.png`, `.qa/v11/bridge-side.png`.

## 8. New QA tool: placement audit

`npm run qa:placement` — walks every placement in `layout.ts`, gives each its
real measured footprint from the GLB on disk, and reports intersections and
grounding failures. Rotation-aware and asymmetry-aware (the hero's entrance
forecourt extends 18.5 m one way and 14.6 m the other; treating it as a centred
box misplaces it by metres).

It earned its place immediately. It found:

- **four yard props buried inside the enlarged hero podium** — invisible in
  triangle counts and from most camera angles, exactly the §23 defect class;
- a cone sitting inside the boundary wall;
- and then, once the hero's true position was used, that
  `scripts/qa/world-shots.mjs` and `HeroBuilding.tsx` **disagreed about where
  the hero building is by 8.5 m**. The QA frames had been rendering a scene
  that did not exist. Both now read `HERO_BUILDING.modelZOffset`.

Final state: **61 placed objects, no intersections, no floating or sunken
placements.**

| | Before V11 | After V11 | Δ |

| Hero building | 55,840 tris / 2.69 MB | 62,180 tris / 3.04 MB | +11 % tris |

Each rebuilt asset costs more triangles than it did — that is what the added
architecture buys. But the **heaviest frame got 7 % lighter**, because the V11

fewer punched openings overall. Draw calls are flat. Bundle unchanged at
87.5 kB shared / 403 kB first load.

No LOD pair was created for the external car (§32) — it is 4.1 MB and appears
at most 3 times, never in the foreground now. That is a defensible cost, and
§32 says to split only where mobile profiling justifies it. Recorded as a
watch item, not a fix.

## 14. Remaining defects

Recorded honestly rather than closed.

1. **Site props are one model.** Eight cones, no drums or pallets. Varied in
   rotation and scale, but a second and third prop class would be a genuine
   improvement. Needs a developer to drop two more GLBs in — the pipeline will
   pick them up with no code change.
2. **Road scores 8.9 against a 9.0 target.** Aggregate, shoulder and wear are
   good; I did not add new road work this pass, so I am not claiming the point.
3. **External car is 4.1 MB** with no LOD pair. Justified while it stays out of
   the foreground; revisit if mobile profiling flags it.
4. **`npm run lint` is unconfigured** in this repo and drops into an
   interactive ESLint setup prompt. Pre-existing; not silently changed.
5. **The concept car remains the only external passenger vehicle.** A typical
   Indian hatchback or sedan GLB would let it retire to the background
   entirely.
